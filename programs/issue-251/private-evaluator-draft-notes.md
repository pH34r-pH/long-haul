# Private evaluator draft: remaining security boundaries

This local branch is an experimental prototype for #245. It is not an admitted worker or evidence profile and must not be used for private tasks or merged as complete.

- Existing v1 evaluator and repository-work fingerprints can commit to low-entropy sealed inputs or outputs. Removing those hashes from the worker prompt in #259 is a narrow mitigation, not a complete private-evidence boundary. Historical v1 evidence must remain readable, but newly emitted raw v1 packets must not be assumed public-safe.
- The v2 prototype uses independent keyed commitments for every non-public check, including policy and inspection checks, and emits a redacted summary. A new experimental Key Vault adapter generates keys in trusted control, binds an immutable secret version, verifies readback, and requests zero SDK retries. Tests use a fake client and pinned SDK response/retry models offline; they do not qualify a live service. No actual vault, authenticated principal, SDK diagnostics policy, durable pre-write recovery receipt, key-disable/delete/purge route, or protected deployment has been qualified. Revocation and storage operations are not atomic. The adapter is not admitted for private tasks.
- The experimental sink now requires an exact trusted-control registry entry and a current resource-policy retention decision before and after evaluation. The callback remains trusted control code: this does not authenticate its registration, prove durable private persistence, or constrain what the callback does. Integrate a real authenticated private persistence authority before use, and qualify invalid/interrupted attempts, storage failures, retention, revocation and deletion.
- The prototype keys candidate, task, contract, environment and sandbox bindings in its public summary, as well as the private profile's public-evaluation reference. Public identifiers and metadata still require explicit classification, and every event, package and worker projection must be tested together for low-entropy enumeration and payload disclosure. Binding and safe-summary verification alone do not provide authentication.
- Exercise malicious sink, stale policy, revoked or rotated keys, forged or replayed summaries, changed candidate/environment, denied store access, cleanup failures and crash recovery. Do not treat fake sandbox tests as gVisor qualification.
- Do not emit raw private hashes, hidden inputs, diagnostics, output digests, private paths or key material in public events or model-visible surfaces. Preserve existing public/historical v1 evidence without silently relabelling it private-safe.
- The worker's current writable bind mount has no live byte or inode quota. Preliminary bounded seed and Docker-copy archive components do not authorize input, are not connected to worker execution and do not prove a safe snapshot. A local Docker probe showed a tmpfs volume needs a running holder across worker stop, and seed permissions must accommodate the unprivileged worker. Holder and volume identity, isolation and crash cleanup, paused-container identity, copy subprocess timeout and successful exit, interlock integration, adversarial gVisor qualification and cleanup/recovery remain required.
- The holder allocator, pre-allocation intent, cleanup and exact-readback seeder are experimental components. A private local-root lock and durable active marker fence cooperating controllers on one host, but the scheduler must bind every controller for the same workspace to that exact root, fence stale or remote controllers, and hold its delivery lease through cleanup. Seed-policy admission remains trusted control and is not atomic policy revocation. Crash recovery must retain the lease until all resources and private data are reclaimed.
- The fenced local intent can own and recover a private scratch directory. Candidate Git freeze and evaluator materialization accept an explicit scratch root, but the actual Pi/evaluator orchestration does not yet bind those calls to the same live fence. The worker and evaluator lifecycle must keep the fence until private scratch is reclaimed; an optional path alone is not crash-safe integration.
- `QuotaPiWorker.run_bounded` is an experimental actual-Pi integration using the local fence, holder, seeder and paused views. It does not replace the default worker, forbids direct event-store writes, and leaves the fence held through caller-owned evaluation and cleanup. The caller must prevent publication or promotion until exact cleanup and evidence admission are proved. Distributed scheduler fencing, whole-operation deadlines, concurrent revocation, crash/recovery and real private authority remain unqualified.

See #245 and #252 for acceptance criteria and current program evidence.
